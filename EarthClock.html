About EarthClock

A to-scale-ish model of Earth's spin, its 23.5° axial tilt, and its trip around the Sun with the Moon in tow — the three things that actually cause day/night, time zones and the seasons.

  • Sizes and distances are schematic, not to scale — a real Earth–Sun gap would make the Earth invisible at this zoom. The tilt angle (23.5°) and the shapes of the orbits are kept accurate.
  • Local Solar Time is true solar time at your chosen longitude (the Sun is exactly overhead at 12:00:00) — it can differ from your official clock time by up to roughly an hour or more, since real time zones follow political borders, not longitude.
  • Equator — 0° latitude.
  • Tropic of Cancer / Capricorn — ±23.5°, where the Sun sits directly overhead at the June and December solstices. These are the "season lines."
  • Arctic / Antarctic Circle — ±66.5°, the edge of the 24-hour day/night zone at the solstices.
  • This page can't use device GPS, so set your location by clicking the globe, searching a city, typing coordinates, or using your device's time zone as a rough guess.
